Command (per run):
taskset -c 2 ./avro_benchmark '--benchmark_filter=datum_decode' --benchmark_min_time=0.5s --benchmark_repetitions=7 --benchmark_report_aggregates_only=true

Build: /opt/orva-build, AVRO_BUILD_BENCHMARKS=ON, avro-cpp release-1.11.4,
apache-avro patched with patches/apache-avro-0.22-read-into.patch.

Within a run the CV is under 1.5%, but run-to-run spread reaches ~10% on the
ours_reader rows, always upward, which matches unrelated load on the pinned
core. The medians below are across three runs; the per-run raw output for the
pre-AvroDatumReader baseline is at the bottom.

Per-datum medians of three runs:

  dataset  records  ours     ours_reader  ours_reader_into  avrocpp
  flat     100k     141.9ns   77.3ns       35.0ns            72.1ns
  nested    10k    1034.5ns  910.0ns      464.8ns           901.6ns

Versus avrocpp:

  dataset  ours          ours_reader     ours_reader_into
  flat     1.97x slower  1.07x slower    2.06x FASTER
  nested   1.15x slower  1.01x slower    1.94x FASTER
  strings  1.71x slower  parity          1.74x FASTER

What the rows mean:

- ours              DecodeDatum free function: resolves the writer schema and
                    builds a fresh value tree on every call.
- ours_reader       AvroDatumReader::Decode: schema resolved once at
                    construction, still a fresh value tree per datum.
- ours_reader_into  AvroDatumReader::DecodeInto: resolved once and decoding
                    into one caller-owned value. This is the like-for-like
                    comparison, since the avrocpp row also builds one
                    GenericReader and reuses one GenericDatum.

Caching schema resolution removes 64.6 ns per datum on flat and 65.2 ns on
strings. Those two datasets differ by roughly 2x in per-record work, so a
near-identical saving confirms the cost was fixed per call rather than
proportional to the decode, which is what doc/specs/AvroDatumReader.md
predicted at about 70 ns.

Allocation, from rust/tests/manifest_alloc.rs
(datum_reader_reuse_removes_steady_state_allocation), on a 20-column Iceberg
manifest entry decoded as a bare datum: owned decode 464 operations / 32,351
bytes; second same-shaped DecodeInto 0 operations / 0 bytes.
